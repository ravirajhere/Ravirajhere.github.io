🌅 Chapter 1: The Beginning (2008)

🔊 Listen to this chapter
Playing...
Fig. 1.1: My childhood photo

The story begins in March 2008 — the day I was born.

It was a Wednesday.

On the 6th day, Mummy decided to name me with a letter — 'R'.

But her mind wasn't made up yet. So people around started suggesting names.

Nishant. Chinmay. Blah blah...

Days passed. And everyone started calling me 'Ghantee' 😏

Actually, I am the third child — after two sisters. But in Indian culture, if the third child is a son, he gets a different level of love.

Since childhood, I was a chubby kid. But because of my habits, I would fall sick every month.

Because of that, I started getting thin — and my face started looking real.

"A story begins not with a date, but with a heartbeat." — Ravi Raj

🕊️ Chapter 2: A Loss and a New Beginning (2009)

🔊 Listen to this chapter
Playing...
Fig. 2.1: Ravi Raj in 2010s

The first picture I have is from 2009 — the year my ration card was made.

It was winter. Papa and Mummy set up a curtain in the background, and all five of us — the whole family — got our photo clicked together.

I never made direct eye contact with anyone. That's what Mummy told me later.

In 2009 itself, on 12th January, my Dadi ji passed away. Brain hemorrhage.

I don't remember anything — I was only 11 months old.

But now, when I look back, I realize something that stings: I never got my Dadi's love. Not even a single memory of it.

Anyway. Life kept moving.

That same year, my elder sister — Richa di — got enrolled in a newly opened school called GBGS (Gautam Buddha Global School).

"Loss teaches us what love truly means." — Ravi Raj

🏠 Chapter 3: A Changing Home (2010–2012)

🔊 Listen to this chapter
Playing...
Fig. 3.1: 4th Birthday - 2012

Days kept passing. And 2009 brought a lot of problems.

First, Mummy's second issue. Then, Dadi's death.

2010 — for me, or for my family — must have been better. That year, there was a wedding in the family. And Bua ji's son was born too, I think.

2011 was similar. When you shift to a new house, you have to visit old relatives for pooja and all. So everyone would call out my name from the road itself.

And I would secretly watch those people.

That year, my Mundan happened — 4 May 2012. I obviously wasn't happy about it. My dear hair was gone.

In 2012, my first birthday was probably celebrated on 26 March. I had perhaps turned 4.

They say when new hair grows, the mind grows too. So after my Mundan, I got attached to Anganwadi — Aunty's school. There, I would count and eat soya beans.

I would stay there till the very end. And somehow, without even realizing it, I finished the Nursery syllabus.

"Swinging on the jhula has been a part of my childhood since the very beginning."

"Home is not a place — it's the people who wait for you." — Ravi Raj

2014 — I moved to UKG.

In 2015, I was in 1st Class. Mummy taught at Don Bosco Academy. Due to financial constraints, I started going with her to the Don Bosco Academy branch — Cheria Bariyarpur, Begusarai. Because it was free for me there.

I went there for only 3 months. The travel itself used to cause bone trouble. And we would reach home only by evening.

At that time, there was a dispute in our house. A property division happened — of land, belongings, everything.

Though our house was very big, and it was already a 2-sided house, so there was no problem.

Earlier, I used to live on the left side. Then I started living on the new right side.

But the financial condition kept getting worse.

In 2016, after 3 months, I continued again at GBGS.

In 2016, I did not appear for the Term-2 exam of 1st Class. My health had deteriorated. I was unhealthy.

'An 8-year-old boy'

Friends' names: Abhilash, Ayush, Harsh.

"Sometimes a family breaks, but love never truly leaves." — Ravi Raj

✨ Chapter 9: A Dream Takes Shape (2018–2019)

🔊 Listen to this chapter
Playing...
📸 Upload Chapter Photo Fig. 9.1: Add your photo description here

My Mummy joined GBGS in 2018 itself. From then on, she taught Hindi in this school.

Time kept passing. And exams kept happening.

Our school had good "Sakhi" — current affairs and all. Everyone was very much into it.

That school was only up to 8th. But students could only reach up to 6th-7th. Because after that, they would leave. There were no teachers.

So according to that, we — the ones in 5th — became the seniors. So we 4 friends would go to conduct the assembly.

And a girls' group + 4-5 more girls would do the assembly.

Even in commanding, we had fights. "I'll do it" — "No, I'll do it"... Bla Bla Bla.

I participated in the whole function for the first time. And that too in 5th Class.

And at that time, whatever was my dream of becoming an Engineer — that dream was going to come true anyway.

This was the first time — on 15 August 2018 — I participated in a dance and also gave a speech.

My uncle (Director Sir) gifted me a Pen. And in the assembly, he asked every child: "What will you become when you grow up?"

Someone said Doctor. Someone said Police, Army, Astronaut, Scientist...

But I — Suraj Anand (Ravi Raj) — went up and said "Engineer".

Then Sir asked: "Which one — Bridge Engineer or Computer Engineer?"

And I went silent.

Because I didn't even know what it takes to become an engineer!

"Dreams don't shout — they quietly take shape." — Ravi Raj

🕯️ Chapter 10: A Tribute and a Farewell (2019)

🔊 Listen to this chapter
Playing...
📸 Upload Chapter Photo Fig. 10.1: Add your photo description here

I just wanted to become an engineer because my Mummy ji was an engineer.

Anyway. In March 2019, my 5th Class exam happened. But just before that — on 10 Feb 2019, Saraswati Puja — something special happened.

In between, in Nov 2018 itself, an EC Competition (Clean & Green School) exam happened on the 14th, at Don Bosco.

It was the first time I went from home with the school kids. With friends, I had fun.

When the result came out, I realized for the first time what a competition really was. My cutoff was 85/100.

Out of them, only 51 children were selected. And the highest marks were 96.

In the evening, the management called us and gave awards to everyone. Certificate, medal, and a GK Award.

I felt happy that for the first time I got a gift and so much respect. But I felt bad that the competition was of L.K.G. level — so how would I compete?

However, on 14 Feb 2019, the Pulwama attack also happened. And the prize distribution was on the same day.

So I paid tribute to the CRPF Indian Forces. And then came back.

The final exam of Class 5th was of ICSE pattern. The result was supposed to come in the last week of March — on 31/03/2019.

On the first day of the class, 85%+ and second position was held by Sushil — who was my competitor.

My mood had changed. And I became diligent (hardworking) too.

My Mummy always thought well about me. And when she found out that I passed 5th Class, her happiness knew no bounds.

Since I had shifted schools, I made friends with everyone. Those who scored more than 80% were very few.

When the talk of our 8 mates came up — everyone left the school. Everyone got separated. Everyone started handling themselves.

What had to happen — started happening...

"Some farewells teach us how to say hello to a new self." — Ravi Raj
